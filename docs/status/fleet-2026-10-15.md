# R10 first implementation: shared-world fleet and ground swarm

October 15 continuation from `59661cb`, Jetson aarch64 / ROS 2 Humble / Gazebo
Fortress 6. The latest instruction puts the R10 extension first, followed by
the existing roadmap. R10 remains partial; implementation and scoped runtime
measurements are separate from the remaining acceptance campaigns.

## Implemented

- One selected physical world and ROS clock, 1–8 named Bumperbot/Labbot instances,
  independent source wheel controllers/joint states/sensors and prefixed TF.
  Actual inter-robot collision geometry is retained, without duplicate worlds.
- Native **Fleet & Swarm** GUI: installed-map/member editor, save/load, command
  autofill, latched single/group Drive, calibrated WASD/gamepad inputs, All Stop,
  real Nav2 goal dispatch/nearest-idle allocation and pause/resume/cancel barriers.
- One shared map, per-instance AMCL/Smac2D/RPP; independent measured state and
  source/scene manifests and bounded SSD journals. Battery/infrastructure unknown.
- Four explicitly educational ground group kernels: formation, rendezvous,
  dispersion and flocking. Fresh clock, stamped wheel/scan/body observations,
  localization and operator heartbeat precede commands; obstacle/separation
  guards, leave/rejoin and actual group Stop are implemented.
- Scoped reset stops/cancels the member, waits for pose-derived physical
  settling and restores its measured spawn. Wheel history/world time continue;
  only that member's anchor/localization is reconciled. No motion controller
  writes body poses; Reset is an explicit operator operation.

The first-party Labbot fleet derivative lifts its actual lidar 5 cm above the
source camera visual, resolving the measured self-return obstruction. Original
single-robot assets and their certificates are unchanged. This adjustment is
part of the new source identity, not an extension of old qualification.

## Real scoped diagnostic

The normal GUI constructed the command and drove **alpha/Bumperbot and
beta/Labbot together in nav_empty/Gazebo**, navigation enabled, distinct ROS
domain 188 and transport partition. No poses were written during movement.

| Observation | Measured result |
|---|---|
| Manual arm without input | 0 m Bumperbot displacement during the 2 s interval |
| Independent manual motion | Bumperbot 0.76880 m forward; Labbot 0.71445 m reverse; Labbot stayed at its pose while Bumperbot drove |
| Nav2 goal `(0.9, -0.5, 0)` | Actual action returned success; physical position error **0.06218 m** and yaw error **0.000579 rad** at the result |
| Six-second formation request | Both bodies physically turned/translated through wheel commands; convergence not assessed |

The Nav2 result was observed while alpha still rotated at **0.40553 rad/s**.
Formation was requested next. The diagnostic establishes dispatch/feedback
wiring and the measured endpoint; it does **not** establish a settled physical
arrival or full mission acceptance. Minimum sampled centre separation was
0.8499997 m; no inter-robot contact was deliberately exercised or instrumented.

## Remaining implementation and validation

R10.1/2/3/8 are partial. Remaining initial implementations include a real task
queue, Open-RMF traffic/resource adapters, cooperative coverage, distinct-FCU
aerial swarms, shared-world adapters for the other backends and additional
robot families. Marine robots/maps/sensing and six/eight/many-legged controllers
are queued next, ahead of the old R3–R9 work. Complete models must be provisioned
into normal GUI/registry workflows, not download-only entries.

The later validation lane needs settled arrivals, physical pause/cancel/resume
and late acknowledgements, real peer sensor/process loss and joins, contacts,
dynamic reset destinations, repeat/world coverage, 3–8 members and long-running
performance. A template count, source-profile match, group kernel or isolated
experiment job is not that acceptance.
